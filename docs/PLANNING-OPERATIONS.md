# Operations planning tools

These 100 commands run offline, print JSON `{result,note}`, and never contact a bridge, call a model, acquire control, or execute actions. Every rate, density, delay, cooldown, and scale is supplied by the caller in consistent units. Reports are idealized arithmetic: they do not establish game mechanics, predict outcomes, or guarantee that an operation is safe or successful.

Arguments: `n` signed number, `q` nonnegative, `p` positive, `i` whole signed, `c` whole nonnegative, `s` whole positive, `r` fraction in [0,1]. CSV lists use `l` signed, `L` nonnegative, `P` positive, `C` whole nonnegative, `S` whole positive. `points2` and `points3` are JSON arrays of `[x,z]` or `[x,y,z]` rows passed as one quoted argument. Numeric magnitudes are at most 1,000,000; lists, point arrays, and generated sequences have at most 1,000 entries. Multi-list calculations require matching lengths unless documented otherwise. Whole-unit rounding tolerates only narrow floating-point roundoff next to a nonzero integer, using the shared resource rounding policy. Nonfinite or unsafe-range results fail without a success report. Use `--` before positional arguments that start with a minus sign.

Every planner here also works with all nine [study workflows](PLANNING-STUDIES.md); run `mc-agent study catalog GROUP` for its exact argument types.

## `stripmine`

Offline branch-mining layout arithmetic from supplied corridor sizes, spacings, and densities. Exposure and yield are idealized estimates; no ore distribution, terrain, or hazard is inspected.

| # | Command | Input types | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `branch-count <corridor-length> <lead> <branch-width> <gap> <sides>` | `s,c,s,c,s` | Count the branches that fit along a corridor as sides (1 or 2) times the largest whole count per side, possibly zero, whose last branch ends at or before the last corridor cell when the first branch starts right after the lead, rejecting a lead longer than the corridor. |
| 2 | `branch-starts <branches-per-side> <lead> <branch-width> <gap>` | `s,c,s,c` | List, for branches one to branches-per-side in order from the entrance, the one-based corridor cell index of the first cell of each branch, which is lead plus (index minus one) times the pitch plus one. |
| 3 | `comb-footprint <branches-per-side> <lead> <branch-width> <gap> <branch-length> <corridor-width> <sides>` | `s,c,s,c,s,s,s` | Report the bounding rectangle of a comb as alongCells (lead plus every branch width on one side plus the gaps between those branches), acrossCells (corridor width plus sides, 1 or 2, times branch length), and areaCells (their product). |
| 4 | `comb-cells <corridor-length> <corridor-width> <height> <branches> <branch-width> <branch-length>` | `s,s,s,c,s,s` | Count excavated cells as corridorCells (corridor length times corridor width times height), branchCells (branches, the total over both sides, times branch width times branch length times height), and totalCells (their sum), rejecting branches whose larger side, half the branches rounded up, is wider in total than the corridor length. |
| 5 | `comb-faces <corridor-length> <corridor-width> <height> <branches> <branch-width> <branch-length>` | `s,s,s,c,s,s` | Count the solid unit faces bounding a comb as floorFaces and ceilingFaces (each corridor length times corridor width plus branches times branch width times branch length), wallFaces (both corridor side walls less the branch mouths, plus the corridor far end, plus two side walls and one end per branch, with the entrance end open), and totalFaces (their sum), assuming at least one solid cell between neighboring branches on a side and rejecting branches whose larger side, half the branches rounded up, cannot fit along the corridor length that way. |
| 6 | `comb-reveal <branches> <branch-width> <height> <branch-length> <gap> <side-reveal> <vertical-reveal>` | `s,s,s,s,c,c,c` | Count the unique cells revealed by one row of branches as sideCells (height times branch length times the sum of twice the side reveal and, for each of the branches minus one gaps, the smaller of the gap and twice the side reveal), verticalCells (twice the vertical reveal times branch width times branch length times branches), and totalCells (their sum). |
| 7 | `pitch-efficiency <branch-width> <height> <gap> <side-reveal> <vertical-reveal>` | `s,s,c,c,c` | Report the unique revealed cells per dug cell for one interior branch of a long row, which is height times the smaller of the gap and twice the side reveal, plus twice the vertical reveal times branch width, all divided by branch width times height. |
| 8 | `layer-coverage <branch-width> <gap> <side-reveal>` | `s,c,c` | Split one pitch of a long branch row into dugFraction (branch width over pitch), revealedFraction (the smaller of the gap and twice the side reveal, over pitch), and unseenFraction (the remaining gap cells over pitch). |
| 9 | `vein-hit-chance <branch-width> <gap> <side-reveal> <vein-extent>` | `s,c,c,s` | Report the fraction of the pitch-many equally likely positions along the corridor axis at which a vein spanning vein-extent consecutive cells of that axis has at least one cell dug or side-revealed by a long branch row, which is one minus the larger of zero and gap minus twice the side reveal minus vein-extent plus one, over the pitch. |
| 10 | `widest-gap <branch-width> <side-reveal> <vein-extent> <target-chance>` | `s,c,s,r` | Find the largest whole gap whose vein-hit-chance is at least a positive target chance, which is the sum of branch width, twice the side reveal, and vein-extent minus one, divided by the target and rounded down, minus branch width. |
| 11 | `stacked-coverage <branch-width> <gap> <height> <vertical-gap> <side-reveal> <vertical-reveal> <stagger>` | `s,c,s,c,c,c,c` | Report the observed fraction of the cells in one repeating block (pitch columns by twice the layer step in rows) of the stacked cross-section whose every second layer is shifted along the corridor by a stagger smaller than the pitch, rejecting blocks of more than 1,000,000 cells. |
| 12 | `layer-plan <layers> <height> <vertical-gap> <branch-width> <gap>` | `s,s,c,s,c` | List stacked layers from the lowest up as rows with layer (one-based), floorOffset (layer minus one, times the sum of height and vertical gap), and alongShift (zero for odd layers and half the pitch rounded down for even layers). |
| 13 | `expected-finds <dug-cells> <revealed-cells> <density>` | `c,c,q` | Multiply the observed cells (dug plus revealed) by a supplied density of expected finds per observed cell. |
| 14 | `cells-for-finds <target-finds> <density> <revealed-per-dug>` | `q,p,q` | Count the cells to dig, rounded up, so that the observed cells (one plus revealed-per-dug for every dug cell) times the density reach the target finds. |
| 15 | `find-rate <dig-time-per-cell> <revealed-per-dug> <density> <period>` | `p,q,q,p` | Report expected finds per period as the period divided by the dig time per cell, times one plus revealed-per-dug, times the density, without rounding to whole cells. |
| 16 | `return-distance <branch-index> <lead> <branch-width> <gap> <branch-length>` | `s,c,s,c,s` | Count the cell steps from the far-end cell of the branch with a one-based index back to corridor cell one, which is branch length plus lead plus (index minus one) times the pitch. |
| 17 | `haul-walk <branches> <lead> <branch-width> <gap> <branch-length> <trips-per-branch>` | `s,c,s,c,s,s` | Report totalWalk (twice the sum of the return distances of branches one to branches, times trips-per-branch, the cell steps of that many round trips between corridor cell one and every far end) and meanReturn (the mean of those return distances). |
| 18 | `branches-within-walk <walk-limit> <lead> <branch-width> <gap> <branch-length>` | `q,c,s,c,s` | Count the leading branches of an unbounded row whose return distance does not exceed the walk limit, which is zero when even branch one is too far. |
| 19 | `branch-time <branch-width> <height> <branch-length> <dig-time-per-cell> <walk-time-per-step>` | `s,s,s,q,q` | Report the time to dig one branch (branch width times height times branch length cells at the dig time per cell) plus the walk from its far end back to the corridor (branch length steps at the walk time per step). |
| 20 | `branches-in-time <time-budget> <dig-time> <first-trip-time> <trip-time-step>` | `q,p,q,q` | Find the largest whole number of branches, possibly zero, whose summed time fits the time budget when branch k (one-based) costs dig-time plus first-trip-time plus (k minus one) times trip-time-step. |
| 21 | `poke-holes <branch-length> <spacing> <poke-depth> <sides> <hole-reveal>` | `s,s,s,s,c` | Report holes (sides, 1 or 2, times branch length divided by spacing and rounded down, one hole per side at every branch cell whose one-based index from the mouth is a multiple of the spacing), dugCells (holes times poke depth), and revealedCells (holes times hole-reveal times the sum of four poke depths and one, with overlaps not removed). |
| 22 | `stair-shaft <depth> <drop-per-step> <width> <headroom>` | `s,s,s,s` | Report steps (depth divided by drop-per-step, rounded up) and cells (width times the sum of depth and steps times headroom) for a descending stair whose every step advances one cell and clears its column from the new tread up to headroom cells above the previous tread, the last step dropping only the remaining depth. |
| 23 | `parallel-combs <strip-width> <corridor-width> <branch-length> <sides> <buffer>` | `s,s,s,s,c` | Report spacing (corridor width plus sides, 1 or 2, times branch length plus buffer, the entrance-to-entrance distance of parallel combs), combs (the most combs, possibly zero, each spacing minus buffer cells wide, that fit across the strip width with one buffer between neighbors), and usedWidth (combs times spacing minus buffer, or zero for no combs). |
| 24 | `corridor-overhead <corridor-width> <branch-width> <gap> <branch-length> <sides>` | `s,s,c,s,s` | Report the fraction of the cells dug per pitch that belong to the corridor, which is corridor width times pitch, over that product plus sides (1 or 2) times branch width times branch length. |
| 25 | `branch-length-for-overhead <corridor-width> <branch-width> <gap> <sides> <target-fraction>` | `s,s,c,s,r` | Find the shortest whole branch length, at least one, whose corridor-overhead does not exceed a positive target fraction. |

Example: `mc-agent stripmine branch-count 41 3 2 5 2` returns `result: 12`.

Conventions: Every size, distance, index, and count is a whole number of unit cells unless a command says otherwise; times, densities, chances, and fractions are real numbers in caller-chosen units. A comb is a straight main corridor, `corridor-length` cells long from the entrance cell (corridor cell one) to its far end, `corridor-width` cells wide and `height` cells tall, with perpendicular dead-end branches of the same height. A branch is `branch-width` cells wide measured along the corridor and `branch-length` cells long measured outward from the corridor side wall, so branch cells never include corridor cells and no junction cell is counted twice. The `gap` is the number of solid cells between consecutive branches on the same side, the pitch is `branch-width` plus `gap`, and the `lead` is the number of corridor cells before the first branch, so branch k (one-based, counted from the entrance) occupies corridor cells lead + (k - 1) × pitch + 1 through lead + (k - 1) × pitch + branch-width. With two sides the branches are mirrored at the same corridor cells; a `sides` value other than 1 or 2 is rejected. `branches` is the total over both sides in `comb-cells` and `comb-faces` and the count on one side everywhere else; those two commands split it as evenly as possible, so the larger side holds half the branches, rounded up, and `comb-cells` rejects that side when its branch widths add up to more than the corridor length. In `comb-faces` every branch keeps both of its side walls, because neighboring branches on a side are assumed to have at least one solid cell between them (touching branches would merge into one wider branch with fewer walls), so the command rejects a larger side whose branch widths plus one cell between each pair of neighbors exceed the corridor length, and wallFaces = 2 × height × corridor-length + corridor-width × height + 2 × branches × height × branch-length. A walking step moves to a face-adjacent cell, and corridor and branch widths are ignored when walking. `side-reveal` is the number of cells beyond each branch side wall that count as revealed, at every level of the branch height and along its whole length; `vertical-reveal` is the number of cells beyond the branch floor and beyond the branch ceiling that count as revealed, over the branch width and length. Nothing is revealed beyond branch ends, diagonally, or by the corridor, and a cell inside the overlapping side reveals of two neighboring branches counts once. Observed cells are dug or revealed cells, `density` is the expected number of finds per observed cell, and `revealed-per-dug` is the number of revealed cells credited to every dug cell. A poke hole is a dead-end hole one cell wide and one cell tall dug `poke-depth` cells into a branch side wall, and `hole-reveal` is the number of cells counted as revealed in each of the four sideways directions from every hole cell and beyond the hole end. For `stacked-coverage` the cross-section perpendicular to the branches is a grid of integer columns x (along the corridor) and rows y (vertical): with pitch = branch-width + gap and step = height + vertical-gap, layer j (every integer, negative included) has dug rows j × step through j × step + height - 1 and dug columns x for which (x - shift) modulo pitch, taken in 0 to pitch - 1, is less than branch-width, where shift is 0 for even j and `stagger` for odd j. A cell is observed when it is dug, when its row is a dug row of some layer and its column is at most side-reveal columns from a dug column of that same layer, or when its column is a dug column of some layer and its row is at most vertical-reveal rows from a dug row of that same layer; the result is the number of observed cells divided by the number of all cells in columns 0 to pitch - 1 and rows 0 to 2 × step - 1, and the command rejects a stagger of pitch or more and a block (pitch × 2 × step) above 1,000,000 cells. Results described as rounded up or rounded down, and the fit test of `branches-in-time`, use the shared whole-unit rounding tolerance, so a quotient within narrow floating-point roundoff of a nonzero whole number is treated as that whole number. `branches-within-walk` rounds the walk limit down to whole steps with that same tolerance and then compares it exactly with the whole return distances, so a limit within roundoff of a return distance admits that branch. `branches-in-time` rejects inputs whose time budget divided by the sum of dig-time and first-trip-time exceeds 9,007,199,254,740,991. A `target-chance` or `target-fraction` of zero is rejected. `branch-starts` and `layer-plan` reject more than 1,000 entries. Object results carry exactly the named fields in the order their description lists them.

Conventions: Animals, pairs, pairings, cycles, and pens are whole counts; feed, times, areas, yields, drops, and rates are real numbers in caller-chosen consistent units. A pair is two adults, so a group of adults forms its count divided by two, rounded down, pairs. A pairing is one pair breeding once: it uses `feed-per-pair` feed and produces `offspring-per-pair` juveniles. The herd is adults plus juveniles, `breeders` are the adults kept for breeding, and no animal dies or leaves except through a cull that a command describes. A projection (`growth-curve`, `age-split`, `cycles-to-harvest`, `feed-to-target`, `feed-budget-cycles`, `founders-for-target`, `fill-pen`, `cull-schedule`) starts with `adults` adults and no juveniles and runs in breeding cycles numbered from one. In cycle k the adults counted at the end of cycle k - 1 (the starting adults for cycle one, and the adults left after the cull in `cull-schedule` and `cycles-to-harvest`) form pairs, every pair breeds once unless the command limits the pairs, and their offspring are born in cycle k. Offspring born in cycle k are juveniles at the end of cycles k through k + maturation-cycles - 1 and adults from the end of cycle k + maturation-cycles on, so they first breed in cycle k + maturation-cycles + 1; with `maturation-cycles` zero they are already adults at the end of their birth cycle. A cycle end is the state after that cycle's births and maturation, and unculled means that no animal is ever removed. `growth-curve`, `age-split`, `cycles-to-harvest`, `feed-budget-cycles`, and `cull-schedule` reject a projection whose herd at the end of any cycle they simulate (before that cycle's cull) exceeds 1,000,000,000,000 animals; the other projections stop before they could reach that size. `feed-to-target` returns null exactly when the starting adults are fewer than both two and `target-herd`. In `feed-budget-cycles` the affordable pairings are feed-budget divided by feed-per-pair, rounded down, and cycles is the largest count n, possibly zero, for which the pairings of cycles one to n total at most the affordable pairings; fewer than two adults (which would breed nothing forever) and an n above 1,000,000 are rejected. `founders-for-target` returns a count from one to `target-herd`. In `fill-pen` the pairs bred in a cycle are the smaller of the pairs the adults form and the room, where the room is capacity minus the herd at the previous cycle end, divided by offspring-per-pair and rounded down (so `fill-pen 5 3 2 52` breeds two pairs in cycle one, not zero); the projection stops at the first cycle in which that is zero, cycles counts the cycles before it, and juveniles count toward the herd. In `cull-schedule` and `cycles-to-harvest` the starting adults all breed in cycle one, even above `keep-adults`, because culls happen only at cycle ends, and culled animals never return. `cycles-to-harvest` runs that projection without a cycle limit until the culled total reaches `harvest`; a cull that leaves fewer than two adults and no juveniles ends all later breeding, maturation, and culling, so the result is null when the total is still short at that point, and a harvest that needs more than 1,000,000 cycles is rejected. `delay-cycles` gives the `maturation-cycles` that match a maturation time when births of cycle k happen at time (k - 1) × cooldown. `growth-factor` solves x^(maturation-cycles + 1) - x^maturation-cycles = offspring-per-pair / 2 for x > 1, which is 1 + offspring-per-pair / 2 when `maturation-cycles` is zero. `steady-herd`, `sustainable-yield`, `breeders-for-demand`, and `harvest-interval` describe a constant group of breeders that breeds once per cycle, one cycle lasting `cooldown`; `period` is the length of the reporting period in the same time unit as `cooldown`, and rates are not rounded to whole animals. In `product-rate` every animal is collected once per visit-interval × (regrow-time divided by visit-interval, rounded up). Pens are rectangles, `area-per-animal` is the pen area required for each animal, and the required area is animals × area-per-animal; `square-pen` and `min-perimeter-pen` return whole side lengths, while `pen-width` and `pen-length` arguments are real lengths. `min-perimeter-pen` considers, for each whole width w, the length that is the larger of w and the required area divided by w, rounded up. `pen-split` uses animals divided by pen-capacity, rounded up, pens; every pen gets animals divided by pens, rounded down, and the first animals modulo pens pens get one more. In `cull-to-fit` the excess is the larger of zero and adults + juveniles - capacity, adultsCulled is the smaller of the excess and the larger of zero and adults - keep-adults, juvenilesCulled is the smaller of juveniles and excess - adultsCulled, breedersCulled is excess - adultsCulled - juvenilesCulled, and herdAfter is adults + juveniles - excess. Divisions of whole counts (pairs, pens, the pairings of `drop-plan`, the cycles of `harvest-interval`) round exactly; every other result described as rounded up or rounded down, including the square root in `square-pen`, uses the shared whole-unit rounding tolerance, so a value within narrow floating-point roundoff of a nonzero whole number is treated as that whole number. `growth-curve` and `cull-schedule` reject more than 1,000 cycles, `pen-split` rejects more than 1,000 pens, `founders-for-target` rejects more than 1,000 cycles, and `mixed-feed` takes two lists of 1 to 1,000 entries and returns pairs and feed in input order. Object results carry exactly the named fields in the order their description lists them.

Conventions: Ticks are caller-chosen time units. A line carries a signal from a source through `wire-cells` wire cells to its end; a booster is one extra cell inserted between two wire cells that restarts the signal, and a run is the consecutive wire cells from the source or a booster to the next booster or the line end, so booster cells are never counted as wire cells and `range` is the most wire cells one run may hold. `line-timing` and `longest-line` use the booster-count boosters, charge `booster-delay` once per booster and `cell-delay` once per wire cell, and add nothing for the source or the line end. `strength-after` and `reach-cells` ignore boosters and lose `loss-per-cell` strength in every wire cell; `strength-after` reports zero once the strength would fall below zero, while `reach-cells` compares the unclamped strength, so a `minimum-strength` of zero counts the cells until the strength first reaches zero (15, 1.5, and 0 give 10). A clock with a `period` and `phase` fires at tick phase + k × period: in `first-coincidence` only for whole k of zero or more, so no tick before the phase counts, while in `high-tick-count` the high-low pattern extends to every whole tick, before the phase too. In `common-period` all clocks fire at tick zero. Every modulo result is taken in 0 to the divisor minus one. `first-coincidence` returns null exactly when the difference of the two phases is not a whole multiple of the greatest common divisor of the periods. In `divider-outputs` the two lists pair entries by position, and with all start counts zero the last output is input-pulses divided by the product of the divisors, rounded down. Wherever a `base` is taken it must be at least 2; base to the power of zero is one, so one state needs zero digits, and `counter-delay` reports zero digits for zero pulses. `digit-encode` and `gray-code` use positional notation with the most significant digit first, and Gray code combines the binary forms of the value and of half the value, rounded down, by exclusive-or. `parity-bit` and `edges` take lists of 0 and 1 entries; `edges` treats the signal before sample zero as 0 and reports zero-based sample indexes. `debounce` accepts repeated equal ticks, and a `hold-ticks` of zero accepts every event. In `sequencer-step` step one begins at tick zero and the steps repeat with a cycle of the summed durations; `step` is one-based, `tickInStep` is from zero to that step duration minus one, and `ticksToNext` is that duration minus `tickInStep`. `level-for-fill` and `fill-for-level` share one rule, so with a `top-level` of one every nonzero fill is level one. Divisions of whole numbers (`booster-count`, `line-timing`, `longest-line` boosters, `divider-outputs`, `counter-wrap`, `level-for-fill`, `fill-for-level`) round exactly. `reach-cells`, `period-for-rate`, and the pulses of `counter-delay` divide real numbers and round with the shared whole-unit rounding tolerance, so a quotient within narrow floating-point roundoff of a nonzero whole number is treated as that whole number, and the fit test of `longest-line` uses the same tolerance, so a delay that exceeds the budget only by such roundoff fits, for every candidate up to and including the true largest count however large the quotient. Because subtracting the minimum from the source strength carries its own roundoff (64.3 minus 64 is not exactly 0.3), `reach-cells` settles the last cell with that same fit test applied to minimum-strength plus cells times loss-per-cell against source-strength, so 64.3, 0.1, and 64 give 3 cells, as by hand. `longest-line` rejects a latency budget divided by cell-delay above 1,000,000,000,000, `counter-delay` rejects more than 1,000,000,000,000 pulses, `common-period` and `counter-wrap` reject a common period or counter capacity above 9,007,199,254,740,991, `digit-encode` rejects a width above 1,000, and other results above 9,007,199,254,740,991 (for example a huge `reach-cells` or `period-for-rate`) fail as unsafe-range results. Lists take 1 to 1,000 entries and list results keep input order unless a command says otherwise. Object results carry exactly the named fields in the order their description lists them.
