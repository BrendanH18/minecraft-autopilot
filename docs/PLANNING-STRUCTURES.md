# Structure planning tools

These 100 commands run offline, print JSON `{result,note}`, and never contact a bridge, call a model, acquire control, or execute actions. Every dimension, spacing, capacity, and rate is supplied by the caller in consistent units (lengths in blocks unless you choose otherwise). Reports are idealized arithmetic: they do not inspect terrain, establish game rules, or guarantee that a structure can be built or used.

Arguments: `n` signed number, `q` nonnegative, `p` positive, `i` whole signed, `c` whole nonnegative, `s` whole positive, `r` fraction in [0,1]. CSV lists use `l` signed, `L` nonnegative, `P` positive, `C` whole nonnegative, `S` whole positive. `points2` and `points3` are JSON arrays of `[x,z]` or `[x,y,z]` rows passed as one quoted argument. Numeric magnitudes are at most 1,000,000; lists, point arrays, and generated sequences have at most 1,000 entries. Multi-list calculations require matching lengths unless documented otherwise. Whole-unit rounding tolerates only narrow floating-point roundoff next to a nonzero integer, using the shared resource rounding policy. Nonfinite or unsafe-range results fail without a success report. Use `--` before positional arguments that start with a minus sign.

Every planner here also works with all nine [study workflows](PLANNING-STUDIES.md); run `mc-agent study catalog GROUP` for its exact argument types.

## `stairplan`

Offline stair, ladder, and ramp arithmetic from supplied rises, runs, and widths. Step sizes are caller assumptions; no terrain, collision, or movement rule is checked.

| # | Command | Input types | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `riser-count <total-rise> <maximum-rise>` | `p,p` | Find the fewest equal risers that climb total-rise with no riser taller than maximum-rise, returning {risers, rise} where risers is total-rise divided by maximum-rise rounded up and rise is total-rise divided by risers. |
| 2 | `riser-options <total-rise> <minimum-rise> <maximum-rise>` | `p,p,p` | Bound the whole numbers of equal risers whose rise lies between minimum-rise and maximum-rise inclusive, returning {fewest, most} where fewest is total-rise divided by maximum-rise rounded up and most is total-rise divided by minimum-rise rounded down, or null when fewest exceeds most. |
| 3 | `flight-size <risers> <rise> <run>` | `s,p,p` | Measure one flight, returning {treads, totalRise, totalRun, stringerLength, pitchDegrees} where treads is risers minus one, totalRise is risers times rise, totalRun is treads times run, stringerLength is the straight distance from the foot of the first riser to the top nosing, and pitchDegrees is the arctangent of rise divided by run. |
| 4 | `flight-in-run <available-run> <rise> <run>` | `q,p,p` | Find the tallest flight whose own treads fit in available-run, returning {risers, totalRise} where risers is one more than available-run divided by run rounded down and totalRise is risers times rise. |
| 5 | `comfort-check <rise> <run> <minimum-stride> <maximum-stride>` | `p,p,q,p` | Test the stride rule, returning {stride, fits} where stride is run plus twice rise and fits is true when stride lies between minimum-stride and maximum-stride inclusive. |
| 6 | `comfort-runs <rise> <minimum-stride> <maximum-stride>` | `p,q,p` | Solve the stride rule for run, returning {minimumRun, maximumRun} where maximumRun is maximum-stride minus twice rise and minimumRun is minimum-stride minus twice rise but never below zero, or null when maximumRun is not positive. |
| 7 | `flight-split <risers> <maximum-per-flight>` | `s,s` | Split risers into the fewest flights of at most maximum-per-flight risers with sizes that differ by at most one, returning {flights, landings, risersPerFlight} where landings is flights minus one and the list runs bottom to top with the larger flights first; at most 1000 flights. |
| 8 | `landing-levels <flight-risers> <rise>` | `S,p` | List the height above the lower floor reached at the top of each listed flight, in the supplied bottom-to-top order, as the running total of risers times rise, so the last entry is the upper floor. |
| 9 | `straight-run <risers> <maximum-per-flight> <tread-run> <landing-depth>` | `s,s,p,q` | Measure a straight stair broken by landings into the fewest flights of at most maximum-per-flight risers, returning {flights, landings, treads, totalRun} where landings is flights minus one, treads is risers minus flights, and totalRun is treads times tread-run plus landings times landing-depth. |
| 10 | `switchback-footprint <flights> <risers-per-flight> <tread-run> <stair-width> <landing-depth> <well-gap>` | `s,s,p,p,p,q` | Size the rectangle covered by a switchback stair whose equal flights alternate between two parallel lanes, returning {length, width} where length is (risers-per-flight minus one) times tread-run, plus landing-depth once for two flights or twice for three or more, and width is stair-width for one flight and otherwise twice stair-width plus well-gap. |
| 11 | `corner-footprint <lower-risers> <upper-risers> <tread-run> <stair-width>` | `s,s,p,p` | Size the rectangle covered by an L-shaped stair with a square corner landing of side stair-width, returning {length, width} where length is (lower-risers minus one) times tread-run plus stair-width and width is (upper-risers minus one) times tread-run plus stair-width. |
| 12 | `opening-length <headroom> <floor-thickness> <rise> <run>` | `p,q,p,p` | Compute how far back from the top nosing the opening in the upper floor must extend so the nosing line passes at least headroom below the underside of that floor, as (headroom plus floor-thickness) times run divided by rise. |
| 13 | `clear-steps <ceiling-height> <headroom> <rise>` | `p,q,p` | Count the steps from the bottom whose tread surface still has at least headroom below a flat ceiling at ceiling-height above the lower floor, as (ceiling-height minus headroom) divided by rise, rounded down. |
| 14 | `step-surface <risers> <rise> <run> <width>` | `s,p,p,p` | Measure the faces of one flight, returning {treadArea, riserArea} where treadArea is (risers minus one) times run times width and riserArea is risers times rise times width. |
| 15 | `under-stair-space <risers> <rise> <run> <width> <tread-thickness> <clear-height>` | `s,p,p,p,q,q` | Measure the usable space beneath a flight, returning {treads, floorArea, volume} over the steps numbered 1 to risers minus one whose underside, at step number times rise minus tread-thickness above the lower floor, is at least clear-height, where floorArea is treads times run times width and volume is the sum of each underside height times run times width. |
| 16 | `rail-posts <risers> <run> <maximum-spacing> <rails>` | `s,p,p,s` | Place rail posts on step nosings at most maximum-spacing apart horizontally, returning {treadInterval, postsPerRail, posts} where treadInterval is maximum-spacing divided by run rounded down, posts stand on step 1, on every treadInterval-th step after it, and on the top step, and posts is postsPerRail times rails. |
| 17 | `lane-count <overall-width> <lane-width> <rail-width> <rails>` | `p,p,q,c` | Count the walking lanes that fit side by side between rails, returning {clearWidth, lanes, spareWidth} where clearWidth is overall-width minus rails times rail-width, lanes is clearWidth divided by lane-width rounded down, and spareWidth is clearWidth minus lanes times lane-width. |
| 18 | `ladder-rungs <height> <rung-spacing> <first-rung-height>` | `p,p,q` | Count ladder rungs placed at first-rung-height and at every rung-spacing above it up to and including height, returning {rungs, topRungHeight} where topRungHeight is first-rung-height plus (rungs minus one) times rung-spacing. |
| 19 | `ladder-sections <height> <maximum-climb> <overlap>` | `s,s,c` | Break a ladder climb into sections of maximum-climb with the remainder on top and a rest platform between consecutive sections, returning {sections, platforms, topSection, ladderLength} where sections is height divided by maximum-climb rounded up, platforms is sections minus one, topSection is the height of the last section, and ladderLength is height plus platforms times overlap. |
| 20 | `leaning-ladder <height> <height-per-offset>` | `p,p` | Set a straight ladder against a wall it touches at height, returning {baseOffset, length, angleDegrees} where baseOffset is height divided by height-per-offset, length is the straight distance from foot to top, and angleDegrees is the arctangent of height-per-offset, measured from the ground. |
| 21 | `ramp-legs <rise> <run-per-rise> <maximum-leg-run> <landing-length>` | `p,p,p,q` | Break a ramp whose run is rise times run-per-rise into the fewest equal legs no longer than maximum-leg-run with a level landing between consecutive legs, returning {legs, landings, legRun, legRise, totalLength} where totalLength is the ramp run plus landings times landing-length. |
| 22 | `two-rise-mix <total-rise> <steps> <low-rise> <high-rise>` | `s,s,s,s` | Find how many of exactly the given number of steps must rise low-rise and how many high-rise to climb total-rise in whole units, returning {lowSteps, highSteps}, or null when no whole mix reaches it exactly. |
| 23 | `common-rise <story-heights> <maximum-rise>` | `S,s` | Find the largest whole rise not above maximum-rise that divides every whole story height exactly, returning {rise, risersPerStory, totalRisers} with risersPerStory in the supplied order and totalRisers their sum. |
| 24 | `climb-time <flight-risers> <seconds-per-riser> <seconds-per-landing>` | `S,q,q` | Total the time to climb the listed flights as the sum of their risers times seconds-per-riser plus seconds-per-landing for each landing between consecutive flights. |
| 25 | `nosing-profile <risers> <rise> <run>` | `s,p,p` | List the nosing of every step from bottom to top as [horizontal, height] rows, with step k at [(k minus one) times run, k times rise]; at most 1000 steps. |

Example: `mc-agent stairplan riser-count 2.75 0.6` returns `result: {"risers": 5, "rise": 0.55}`.

Conventions: Every length uses one caller-chosen unit, so areas and volumes are in that unit squared and cubed; `climb-time` returns the caller's time unit and angles are in degrees. A flight has `risers` equal vertical risers and its steps are numbered from 1 at the bottom: step k has its tread surface k × rise above the lower floor and its nosing (front top edge) (k − 1) × run horizontally from the face of the first riser. The top step of every flight is the upper floor or a landing, so a flight owns risers − 1 treads and covers a horizontal run of (risers − 1) × run; the nosing line is the straight line through the nosings, rising `rise` per `run`. "Rounded up" and "rounded down" mean the ceiling and floor of the stated real quotient after a value within floating-point roundoff of a nonzero whole number is snapped to that number; commands whose inputs are all whole use exact integer arithmetic. A count produced by rounding a real quotient (`risers`, `fewest`, `most`, the `clear-steps` result, `treadInterval`, `lanes`, `rungs`, `legs`) is rejected above 1,000,000, checked on the returned value (so `flight-in-run` risers and `ladder-rungs` rungs are checked after adding one), and in `riser-options` both counts are checked before the null test. Lists run bottom to top and object fields appear in the order written in each row. Limit tests and differences that feed a decision treat two values that differ only by floating-point roundoff as equal: this applies to `fits` in `comfort-check`; to `minimumRun`, `maximumRun`, and `clearWidth`, which are exactly 0 in that case; and to `spareWidth`, which is exactly 0 when lanes × lane-width + rails × rail-width equals overall-width up to roundoff. The `clear-steps` result, `lanes`, and the rungs above the first in `ladder-rungs` round down a quotient of a difference of inputs, whose subtraction roundoff scales with the inputs rather than the quotient, so each limit test settles the last unit: the rounded-down count rises by one when the quotient lies more than one half above it and the next step, lane, or rung passes its limit test up to roundoff, and falls by one when the count itself fails that test. The limit tests are k × rise + headroom at most ceiling-height, lanes × lane-width + rails × rail-width at most overall-width, and first-rung-height + (rungs − 1) × rung-spacing at most height; for example `clear-steps 64.3 64 0.1` is 3 although 64.3 − 64 is slightly below 0.3 in floating point, and the correction never moves a count by more than one unit. Details by command: `flight-size` stringerLength is the square root of totalRise squared plus totalRun squared, and pitchDegrees is measured from the horizontal; `flight-split` and `straight-run` use flights = risers ÷ maximum-per-flight rounded up, and `flight-split` gives the first (risers mod flights) flights one riser more than risers ÷ flights rounded down, which the rest receive; in `switchback-footprint` odd-numbered flights climb one way in one lane and even-numbered flights climb back in the other, landings span both lanes at the ends (a single flight has none), length is measured along the flights and width across the lanes; in `corner-footprint` length is measured along the lower flight and width along the upper flight; `clear-steps` counts step k when k × rise + headroom is at most ceiling-height; `under-stair-space` starts at step max(1, (clear-height + tread-thickness) ÷ rise rounded up) and returns zeros when no step qualifies; `rail-posts` postsPerRail is (risers − 1) ÷ treadInterval rounded up, plus one; `ladder-rungs` rungs is (height − first-rung-height) ÷ rung-spacing rounded down, plus one; `ladder-sections` topSection is height − platforms × maximum-climb; `leaning-ladder` length is the square root of height squared plus baseOffset squared; `ramp-legs` legs is the ramp run ÷ maximum-leg-run rounded up, legRun is the ramp run ÷ legs, and legRise is rise ÷ legs; `two-rise-mix` highSteps is (total-rise − steps × low-rise) ÷ (high-rise − low-rise), which must be whole and between 0 and steps inclusive, and lowSteps is steps − highSteps; `common-rise` always has an answer because 1 divides every height; `climb-time` counts one landing fewer than the number of listed flights. Rejected input: `riser-options` with minimum-rise above maximum-rise; `comfort-check` and `comfort-runs` with minimum-stride above maximum-stride; `flight-split` needing more than 1000 flights; `clear-steps` with headroom above ceiling-height; `rail-posts` with run above maximum-spacing; `lane-count` when rails × rail-width exceeds overall-width; `ladder-rungs` with first-rung-height above height; `two-rise-mix` unless low-rise is below high-rise; `nosing-profile` with more than 1000 risers.

## `roundplan`

Offline counts of integer grid cells in discs, rings, ellipses, cylinders, spheres, domes, and cones sized by whole diameters. A cell counts when its center lies inside the ideal shape; no world blocks are inspected and no particular circle generator is matched.

| # | Command | Input types | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 26 | `disc-cells <diameter>` | `s` | Count the cells inside a disc of the given whole diameter; diameter at most 1000. |
| 27 | `disc-outline <diameter>` | `s` | Count the outline cells of a disc, meaning its inside cells with at least one of the four orthogonal neighbors not inside; diameter at most 1000. |
| 28 | `thick-outline <diameter>` | `s` | Count the inside cells of a disc with at least one of the eight orthogonal or diagonal neighbors not inside, which is the outline with its diagonal steps filled in; diameter at most 1000. |
| 29 | `disc-rows <diameter>` | `s` | List the number of inside cells in each row of a disc, from row 1 to row diameter; diameter at most 1000. |
| 30 | `row-span <diameter> <row>` | `s,s` | Locate the inside cells of one row of a disc, returning {firstCell, lastCell, cells} where firstCell and lastCell are the one-based positions along the row of its first and last inside cells and cells is the number of inside cells, which are contiguous; row is one-based and at most diameter, and diameter is at most 1000. |
| 31 | `row-runs <diameter>` | `s` | Group the rows of a disc, from row 1 onward, into maximal runs of consecutive rows holding the same number of inside cells, returning one [cells, rows] pair per run where cells is that number and rows is the length of the run; diameter at most 1000. |
| 32 | `quadrant-split <diameter>` | `s` | Split a disc along its two center lines, returning {quadrantCells, armCells, centerCells} where quadrantCells counts the inside cells whose x and z center offsets are both positive, armCells counts the inside cells with a zero x offset and a positive z offset, and centerCells counts the inside cells with both offsets zero, so the disc holds four times quadrantCells plus four times armCells plus centerCells; diameter at most 1000. |
| 33 | `square-fill <diameter>` | `s` | Compare a disc with its bounding square of diameter times diameter cells, returning {cornerCells, cellsPerCorner, fillFraction} where cornerCells counts the square cells that are not inside, cellsPerCorner is one quarter of cornerCells, and fillFraction is the inside cells divided by the square cells; diameter at most 1000. |
| 34 | `inscribed-square <diameter>` | `s` | Find the side, in cells, of the largest square block of cells that is centered on the disc center and lies entirely inside the disc; the side has the same parity as diameter, and diameter is at most 1000. |
| 35 | `inscribed-depth <diameter> <width>` | `s,s` | Find the greatest depth, in rows, of a rectangular block of cells that is width cells wide, centered on the disc center, and entirely inside the disc, which equals the number of rows holding at least width inside cells; width is at most diameter and of the same parity, and diameter is at most 1000. |
| 36 | `diameter-for-cells <target-cells>` | `s` | Find the smallest diameter from 1 to 1000 whose disc has at least target-cells inside cells, rejecting a target that the disc of diameter 1000 cannot hold. |
| 37 | `outline-within-budget <available-cells>` | `s` | Find the largest diameter from 1 to 1000 whose disc outline, counted by the four-neighbor rule, has at most available-cells cells. |
| 38 | `ring-bands <diameter> <band-width>` | `s,s` | List the inside cells of a disc in concentric bands band-width cells wide from the rim inward, returning one count per band for bands 1 to diameter divided by twice band-width rounded up, where band k holds the inside cells whose centers lie farther from the disc center than half of diameter minus k times band-width but not farther than half of diameter minus (k minus one) times band-width; diameter at most 1000. |
| 39 | `thick-ring <diameter> <thickness>` | `s,p` | Count the inside cells of a disc whose centers lie farther from the disc center than half of diameter minus thickness, for a real thickness of at most half of diameter; diameter at most 1000. |
| 40 | `ellipse-cells <width> <depth>` | `s,s` | Count the cells inside an ellipse whose bounding grid is width cells along x by depth cells along z; width and depth are each at most 1000. |
| 41 | `ellipse-outline <width> <depth>` | `s,s` | Count the outline cells of an ellipse, meaning its inside cells with at least one of the four orthogonal neighbors not inside; width and depth are each at most 1000. |
| 42 | `cylinder-parts <diameter> <height>` | `s,s` | Break down an upright cylinder of height layers that are each a disc of the given diameter, returning {solidCells, wallCells, capCells, shellCells, hollowCells} where solidCells is every inside cell, wallCells is the disc outline cells of every layer, capCells is the disc cells that are not outline cells in the bottom layer and in the top layer (counted once when height is 1), shellCells is wallCells plus capCells, and hollowCells is solidCells minus shellCells; diameter at most 120. |
| 43 | `sphere-cells <diameter>` | `s` | Count the cells inside a sphere of the given whole diameter; diameter at most 120. |
| 44 | `sphere-shell-cells <diameter>` | `s` | Count the shell cells of a sphere, meaning its inside cells with at least one of the six orthogonal neighbors not inside; diameter at most 120. |
| 45 | `sphere-layers <diameter>` | `s` | List the number of inside cells in each horizontal layer of a sphere, from the bottom layer to the top layer; diameter at most 120. |
| 46 | `shell-layers <diameter>` | `s` | List the number of sphere shell cells, counted by the six-neighbor rule, in each horizontal layer of a sphere, from the bottom layer to the top layer; diameter at most 120. |
| 47 | `dome-parts <diameter>` | `s` | Break down a dome made of the sphere layers whose cell centers are at or above the sphere center, returning {layers, solidCells, roofCells, floorCells, shellCells} where layers is half of diameter rounded up, solidCells is the inside cells of those layers, roofCells is the sphere shell cells of those layers, floorCells is the inside cells of the lowest dome layer that are not sphere shell cells, and shellCells is roofCells plus floorCells; diameter at most 120. |
| 48 | `ellipsoid-cells <width> <height> <depth>` | `s,s,s` | Count the cells inside an ellipsoid whose bounding grid is width cells along x by height cells along y by depth cells along z; each size is at most 120. |
| 49 | `cone-layers <diameter> <height>` | `s,s` | Count the cells of an upright cone layer by layer, returning {layerCells, totalCells} where layerCells lists layers 1 to height from the base upward, a cell of layer k is inside when its center lies within half of diameter times (height minus k plus one half) divided by height of the vertical axis, and totalCells is the sum of the list; diameter at most 120 and height at most 1000. |
| 50 | `profile-cells <layer-diameters>` | `S` | Count the cells of a stack of discs with the listed whole diameters, returning {layerCells, totalCells} where layerCells holds the inside cells of each disc in the supplied order and totalCells is their sum; each diameter is at most 1000. |

Example: `mc-agent roundplan disc-cells 13` returns `result: 137`.

Conventions: Every size is a whole number of cells and every result counts whole cells; the only real-valued result is `fillFraction` and the only real-valued input is the `thick-ring` thickness. A shape of whole diameter d occupies a d-by-d bounding grid (d-by-d-by-d for a sphere), with x and z horizontal and y vertical. Cell index i from 0 to d − 1 on an axis has its center at offset i + 0.5 − d/2 from the shape center on that axis, so an odd diameter is centered on a cell and an even diameter on a cell corner. A cell is inside when the squared distance of its center from the shape center is at most (d/2)², boundary included. Writing u = 2i + 1 − d for twice the offset on each axis (u for x, v for z, w for y), that is the whole-number test u² + v² ≤ d² for a disc and u² + v² + w² ≤ d² for a sphere, and every test in this group is evaluated exactly in such whole numbers rather than in rounded decimals. An ellipse of width a and depth b occupies an a-by-b grid whose cell centers are at x = i + 0.5 − a/2 and z = j + 0.5 − b/2, and a cell is inside when (x/(a/2))² + (z/(b/2))² ≤ 1; an ellipsoid of width a, height c, and depth b occupies an a-by-c-by-b grid with y = k + 0.5 − c/2, and a cell is inside when (x/(a/2))² + (y/(c/2))² + (z/(b/2))² ≤ 1. A row is the set of cells sharing one z index; rows are numbered from 1 (z index 0), and positions along a row count x cells from 1 (x index 0). A layer is the set of cells sharing one y index; layers are numbered from 1 at the bottom (y index 0). Outline cells of a planar shape are its inside cells with at least one of the four orthogonal neighbors not inside, and shell cells of a solid shape are its inside cells with at least one of the six orthogonal neighbors not inside; a cell beyond the bounding grid is never inside, so an inside cell on the edge of the grid always qualifies. Lists are in row order (row 1 first), layer order (bottom layer first), or, for `ring-bands`, band order from the rim inward, and object fields appear in the order written in each row. Convenience forms: `disc-cells d` equals `ellipse-cells d d` and the totalCells of `profile-cells d`, `disc-outline d` equals `ellipse-outline d d`, and `sphere-cells d` equals `ellipsoid-cells d d d`; `disc-cells`, `sphere-cells`, and `sphere-shell-cells` are also the sums of the `disc-rows`, `sphere-layers`, and `shell-layers` lists. Limits: planar sizes (disc diameters, ellipse width and depth, and every `profile-cells` diameter) are at most 1000, and the sizes of solid shapes (cylinder, sphere, dome, and cone diameters and all three ellipsoid sizes) are at most 120; `cylinder-parts` height may be any whole number up to 1,000,000 and `cone-layers` height is at most 1000; larger values are rejected. Details by command: `thick-outline` examines the eight cells that share an edge or a corner with a cell; `disc-rows` has diameter entries and `row-runs` describes the same rows, so its run lengths sum to diameter; in `row-span` every row holds at least one inside cell, firstCell is (diameter − cells) ÷ 2 + 1, lastCell is firstCell + cells − 1, and a row above diameter is rejected; `quadrant-split` gives armCells and centerCells of 0 for an even diameter, where no cell center lies on a center line; `square-fill` cellsPerCorner is always whole; `inscribed-square` returns the largest s of the same parity as diameter with 2 × (s − 1)² ≤ diameter²; `inscribed-depth` returns the largest r of the same parity as diameter with (width − 1)² + (r − 1)² ≤ diameter², and rejects a width above diameter or of the other parity; `diameter-for-cells` rejects a target above 785,456, the cells of the diameter-1000 disc; `outline-within-budget` examines every diameter from 1 to 1000 and returns the largest whose outline fits, so it returns 1000 for any budget of at least 2,828, the outline of the diameter-1000 disc, and diameter 1 (one outline cell) always fits; `ring-bands` with diameter d and band-width b puts an inside cell in band k when u² + v² ≤ (d − 2(k − 1)b)² and, while d − 2kb is positive, u² + v² > (d − 2kb)², so the last band, where d − 2kb is zero or less, keeps every remaining cell including a center cell; the bands sum to `disc-cells d`, the first k bands together equal `thick-ring d` with thickness k × b whenever 2kb ≤ d, a band-width of at least d ÷ 2 gives the single band [`disc-cells d`], any whole band-width is accepted, and a band holding no cells would be listed as 0; `thick-ring` puts an inside cell in the ring when four times its squared center distance, the whole number u² + v², is greater than (diameter − 2 × thickness)², so a cell at exactly half of diameter minus thickness from the center is left out, and a thickness above diameter ÷ 2 is rejected; `cylinder-parts` stacks identical discs in a diameter-by-height-by-diameter grid, capCells is (disc cells − disc outline cells) × min(height, 2), shellCells equals the six-neighbor shell of that solid, and hollowCells is 0 for heights 1 and 2; `dome-parts` keeps sphere layers diameter − layers + 1 to diameter (for an odd diameter the lowest of them is the middle layer of the sphere), roofCells uses the shell of the whole sphere so the roof is open underneath, and shellCells equals the six-neighbor shell of the dome standing alone in its own grid; `cone-layers` uses a diameter-by-height-by-diameter grid with the disc x and z offsets in every layer and an ideal cone whose base circle of the given diameter lies on the bottom face of layer 1 and whose apex lies on the axis at the top face of layer height, so layer k is tested at its mid-height with the exact comparison 4 × height² × (u² + v²) ≤ diameter² × (2 × height − 2k + 1)², and a layer holding no cells is listed as 0; `profile-cells` counts each listed disc independently, so mixed parities are allowed.

## `rampart`

Offline battlement, wall, tower, gate, moat, and earthwork geometry from supplied dimensions. Cell counts are whole unit cubes and other results are ideal continuous geometry; no structural, defensive, or gameplay claim is made.

| # | Command | Input types | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 51 | `merlon-fit <wall-length> <merlon-width> <crenel-width>` | `s,s,s` | Fit the most merlons merlon-width cells wide along wall-length cells when the run starts and ends with a merlon and crenel-width cells separate neighbors, returning {merlons, crenels, leftover} where merlons is wall-length plus crenel-width divided by merlon-width plus crenel-width rounded down, crenels is merlons minus one, and leftover is wall-length less the cells the merlons and crenels use. |

Conventions: Every length uses one caller-chosen unit (blocks unless you choose otherwise), so areas and volumes are in that unit squared and cubed, and every angle is in degrees. A wall run of wall-length cells has cells numbered from 0 at its start, and every listed offset is such a zero-based cell number. The merlon-fit pattern starts and ends with a merlon and alternates merlons and crenels, and every command that uses it rejects a merlon-width greater than wall-length; its leftover cells belong to neither, so `merlon-fit` and `crenellated-parapet` do not depend on where the pattern sits (each crenel notch spans the full parapet thickness), while `merlon-offsets` puts leftover divided by two rounded down unused cells before the first merlon and the rest after the last. The `merlon-fit` merlon count is the `perimeter picket-count` count with whole-cell inputs; `merlon-fit` adds the crenel and leftover cells and fixes the pattern that `merlon-offsets` and `crenellated-parapet` build on. In `battered-courses` only the taper is rounded down, so each course is rounded toward the thicker base: 3 courses from 5 to 4 give [5, 5, 4], not [5, 4, 4]. In `embrasure-cells` every layer widens symmetrically, one cell on each side, so the opening stays centered on the slit; a widen-every of at least wall-thickness gives a straight slit. Rectangles, rings and enclosures are measured on their outer faces and each cell counts once; a ring course of outer size w by d and thickness t holds w times d minus (w minus 2t) times (d minus 2t) cells. Every other command is ideal continuous geometry with no rounding: tower and corbel widths are lengths along a line, `circuit-towers` places tower centers on the circuit corners, moat and glacis slopes are planar and meet along the corner diagonals, the motte is a right circular frustum, the arch is a circular arc of any rise (a rise above half the span gives a horseshoe arch whose arc exceeds 180 degrees), and the drawbridge chain runs straight. The only real-valued rounding is in `circuit-towers`, which rounds each side's gap count up, and `machicolation`, which rounds its corbel count up, both using the shared roundoff tolerance. Lists run in the stated order (bottom to top for `courseThickness`, outer face to inner face for `layerWidths`, innermost ring outward for `ringCells`, increasing offsets for `merlon-offsets` and `loophole-offsets`) and hold at most 1000 entries; larger requests are rejected. Every "must" condition in a description is rejected with an error rather than clamped. Comparisons between computed real quantities treat a difference within the shared roundoff tolerance as equality: a `wall-walk` clear width or `curtain-spans` total curtain that is zero up to roundoff is rejected, an eye that is level with the crest up to roundoff is rejected by `blind-ground`, and a `machicolation` open length that is zero up to roundoff counts as 0 rather than null. The only null result is `machicolation` when its corbel count would overlap the corbels. Results whose whole-cell products exceed the safe integer range fail without a result.

Conventions: A hall is `aisles` parallel aisles numbered from 1 at the door end of a front cross aisle that runs past every aisle mouth. A location is one container position (aisle, side, bay, level) holding one container, so a hall has aisles × 2 × bays-per-side × levels locations; a bay depth is bay number b of both sides of every aisle at every level, 2 × aisles × levels locations. `container-items` is the number of items one container holds (slots-per-container × stack-size for a container of one item type), and items are stored in whole stacks, one stack per slot. The one-based location index runs aisle first, then side, then bay, then level: index = (((aisle − 1) × 2 + side − 1) × bays-per-side + bay − 1) × levels + level, and `location-address` returns the unique (aisle, side, bay, level) with that index. Walking is measured along centerlines in one caller unit with whole `door-offset`, `aisle-pitch`, and `bay-pitch`: the mouth of aisle a lies door-offset + (a − 1) × aisle-pitch from the door along the front cross aisle, the pick point of bay b lies (b − 1/2) × bay-pitch into the aisle from its mouth on the aisle centerline and serves both sides and every level, and the rear cross aisle lies bays-per-side × bay-pitch from the mouth. The bay-walk distance of a location is therefore door-offset + (aisle − 1) × aisle-pitch + (bay − 1/2) × bay-pitch, every distance is an exact multiple of one half, and distance comparisons are exact. Pick tours start and end at the door; pick i is the pair formed by the i-th entries of `pick-aisles` and `pick-bays`, picks are numbered from 1 in list order, and repeated aisles or bays are allowed. In `traversal-route` and `pick-sequence` the distinct listed aisles are visited in ascending order, the j-th of them walked from the front when j is odd and from the rear when j is even, with an odd final aisle entered from the front and left the same way. Item-type lists give type i in entry i, numbered from 1, and lists passed together must have equal lengths. Rankings by picks put larger picks first and keep equal picks in list order. `nearest-slotting` orders all locations of the hall by bay-walk distance and then by location index, so locations at one bay are taken side 1 levels 1 upward, then side 2 levels 1 upward, and equal distances in different aisles go to the lower aisle first. In `abc-classes` the comparisons are strict: a type whose ranked-ahead picks equal a cut times the total does not take that class. `coi-order` compares the quotients exactly by cross multiplication. `door-aisle` crossWalk is the one-way distance along the front cross aisle from the door to each pick's aisle mouth, summed over picks. `aisle-modules` modules do not share racks, and rowsAcross is the number of container positions across the hall width at one bay and level; `hall-footprint` width runs across the aisles and depth along them. In `container-wall` a segment between touching openings, or between an opening and a wall end it touches, has length 0, and openings may touch. `section-fit` numbers sections from 1 in the order they are opened. In `compact-hall` a position is one bay of one aisle holding 2 × levels containers, every aisle count from 1 to positions is examined, and meanWalk is the `mean-walk` mean of the chosen hall. Rounding: commands with whole inputs use exact integer floors and ceilings; the real quotients of `aisle-modules` and the `fill-state` reserve are rounded with the shared policy, which first snaps a value within floating-point roundoff of a nonzero whole number to that number. The levels above the first in `levels-under-ceiling` and each `container-wall` segment count round down a quotient of a difference of inputs, whose subtraction roundoff scales with the inputs rather than the quotient, so each limit test settles the last unit as in `stairplan`: the rounded-down count rises by one when the quotient lies more than one half above it and the next level or column passes its limit test up to roundoff, and falls by one when the count itself fails that test. The limit tests are first-level-height + (levels − 1) × (container-height + level-gap) + container-height at most clear-height, and segment start + columns × container-width at most segment end; for example `levels-under-ceiling 64.3 64 0.1 0` is 3 and `container-wall 64.3 0.1 1 0 64` fits 3 columns after the opening. Comparisons of computed real quantities treat values that differ only by roundoff as equal: this applies to the opening order and wall-end checks of `container-wall`, the level 1 test of `levels-under-ceiling`, the `abc-classes` cut tests, and the `aisle-modules` leftover and `container-wall` segment lengths, which are exactly 0 in that case and never negative. Limits: counts derived from real quotients (`aisle-modules` aisles, `levels-under-ceiling` levels, and each `container-wall` segment count) above 1,000,000 are rejected, `aisle-balance` aisles and `demand-zones` zones are at most 1000, input and output lists hold at most 1000 entries, and results beyond the safe integer range fail without a result. Every input condition a description states with must or after a semicolon, and the index limit of `location-address`, is rejected with an error rather than clamped (the zone cap in `demand-zones` is a clamp), and `door-aisle`, `aisle-balance`, `abc-classes`, and `demand-zones` reject a pick total of 0. Object fields appear in the order written in each row, and lists run in list order unless a row says otherwise. No result is ever null.
